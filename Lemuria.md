An 1876 map illustrating the supposed location of Lemuria and the hypothetical dispersal routes of the "12 [varieties](app://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Race_\(human_categorization\) "Race (human categorization)") of men" from their alleged Lemurian origin across the globe

**Lemuria** (), or **Limuria**, was a [continent](app://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continent "Continent") proposed in 1864 by zoologist [Philip Sclater](app://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philip_Sclater "Philip Sclater"), theorized to have sunk beneath the [Indian Ocean](app://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Ocean "Indian Ocean"), later appropriated by [occultists](app://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Occult "Occult") in supposed accounts of human origins. The theory was discredited with the discovery of [plate tectonics](app://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plate_tectonics "Plate tectonics") and [continental drift](app://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continental_drift "Continental drift") in the 20th century.[[1]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lemuria#cite_note-HSW-1)

The hypothesis was proposed as an explanation for the presence of [lemur](app://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lemur "Lemur") fossils on [Madagascar](app://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madagascar "Madagascar") and the [Indian subcontinent](app://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_subcontinent "Indian subcontinent") but not in continental [Africa](app://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Africa "Africa") or the [Middle East](app://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_East "Middle East"). Biologist [Ernst Haeckel](app://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ernst_Haeckel "Ernst Haeckel")'s suggestion in 1870 that Lemuria could be the ancestral home of humans caused the hypothesis to move beyond the scope of [geology](app://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geology "Geology") and [zoogeography](app://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zoogeography "Zoogeography"), ensuring its popularity outside of the framework of the scientific community.

During the latter part of the 19th century, [Helena Blavatsky](app://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helena_Blavatsky "Helena Blavatsky"), occultist and founder of [theosophy](app://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theosophy "Theosophy"), placed Lemuria in the system of her mystical-religious doctrine, claiming that this continent was the homeland of the human ancestors, whom she called Lemurians. The writings of Blavatsky had a significant impact on Western [esotericism](app://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_esotericism "Western esotericism"), popularizing the myth of Lemuria and its mystical inhabitants.

Theories about Lemuria became untenable when, in the 1960s, the scientific community accepted [Alfred Wegener](app://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfred_Wegener "Alfred Wegener")'s theory of [continental drift](app://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continental_drift "Continental drift"), presented in 1912, but the idea lived on in the popular imagination, especially in relation to the Theosophist tradition.

Lemuria was hypothesized as a [land bridge](app://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Land_bridge "Land bridge"), now sunken, which would account for certain discontinuities in [biogeography](app://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biogeography "Biogeography"). This idea has been rendered obsolete by modern theories of [plate tectonics](app://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plate_tectonics "Plate tectonics"). Sunken continents such as [Zealandia](app://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zealandia "Zealandia") in the Pacific, and [Mauritia](app://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mauritia_\(microcontinent\) "Mauritia (microcontinent)")[[2]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lemuria#cite_note-bbc.co.uk-2) and the [Kerguelen Plateau](app://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kerguelen_Plateau "Kerguelen Plateau") in the Indian Ocean do exist,[[3]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lemuria#cite_note-3) but no geological formation under the Indian or Pacific oceans is known that could have served as a land bridge between these continents.[_[citation needed](app://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")_]

In 1864, "The Mammals of Madagascar" by [zoologist](app://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zoologist "Zoologist") and biogeographer [Philip Sclater](app://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philip_Sclater "Philip Sclater") appeared in _[The Quarterly Journal of Science](app://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Quarterly_Journal_of_Science "The Quarterly Journal of Science")_. Using a classification he referred to as [lemurs](app://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lemur "Lemur"), but which included related primate groups,[[4]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lemuria#cite_note-Neild,_Ted_2007_pp._38%E2%80%9339-4) and puzzled by the presence of their [fossils](app://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fossil "Fossil") in Madagascar and India, but not in Africa or the Middle East, Sclater proposed that Madagascar and India had once been part of a larger continent (he was correct in this; though in reality this was [Mauritia](app://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mauritia_\(microcontinent\) "Mauritia (microcontinent)")[[5]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lemuria#cite_note-5) and the [supercontinent](app://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supercontinent "Supercontinent") [Gondwana](app://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gondwana "Gondwana")).

> The anomalies of the mammal fauna of Madagascar can best be explained by supposing that... a large continent occupied parts of the Atlantic and Indian Oceans... that this continent was broken up into islands, of which some have become amalgamated with... Africa, some... with what is now Asia; and that in Madagascar and the [Mascarene Islands](app://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mascarene_Islands "Mascarene Islands") we have existing relics of this great continent, for which... I should propose the name Lemuria![[4]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lemuria#cite_note-Neild,_Ted_2007_pp._38%E2%80%9339-4)

Sclater's theory was hardly unusual for his time; "[land bridges](app://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Land_bridge "Land bridge")", real and imagined, fascinated several of Sclater's contemporaries. [Étienne Geoffroy Saint-Hilaire](app://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%89tienne_Geoffroy_Saint-Hilaire "Étienne Geoffroy Saint-Hilaire"), also looking at the relationship between animals in India and Madagascar, had suggested a southern continent about two decades before Sclater, but did not give it a name.[[6]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lemuria#cite_note-6) The acceptance of [Darwinism](app://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darwinism "Darwinism") led scientists to seek to trace the diffusion of species from their points of [evolutionary](app://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evolution "Evolution") origin. Before the acceptance of [continental drift](app://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continental_drift "Continental drift"), biologists frequently postulated the existence of submerged land masses to account for populations of land-based species now separated by barriers of water. Similarly, geologists tried to account for striking resemblances of rock formations on different continents. The first systematic attempt was made by [Melchior Neumayr](app://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melchior_Neumayr "Melchior Neumayr") in his book _Erdgeschichte_ in 1886. Many hypothetical submerged land bridges and continents were proposed during the 19th century to account for the present distribution of species.

[![](app://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/a5/Coat_of_arms_of_the_British_Indian_Ocean_Territory.svg/250px-Coat_of_arms_of_the_British_Indian_Ocean_Territory.svg.png)](app://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Coat_of_arms_of_the_British_Indian_Ocean_Territory.svg)

The [coat of arms of the British Indian Ocean Territory](app://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coat_of_arms_of_the_British_Indian_Ocean_Territory "Coat of arms of the British Indian Ocean Territory") with the inscription (in Latin) "Limuria is in our charge/trust".

After gaining some acceptance within the scientific community, the concept of Lemuria began to appear in the works of other scholars. [Ernst Haeckel](app://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ernst_Haeckel "Ernst Haeckel"), a Darwinian [taxonomist](app://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taxonomist "Taxonomist"), proposed Lemuria as an explanation for the absence of proto-human "[missing links](app://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transitional_fossil#Missing_links "Transitional fossil")" in the fossil record. According to another source, Haeckel put forward this thesis before Sclater, without using the name "Lemuria".[[7]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lemuria#cite_note-7)

The Lemuria theory disappeared completely from conventional scientific consideration after the theories of [plate tectonics](app://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plate_tectonics "Plate tectonics") and [continental drift](app://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continental_drift "Continental drift") were accepted by the larger scientific community. According to the theory of plate tectonics, Madagascar and India were once part of the same landmass, thus accounting for geological resemblances, but plate movement caused India to break away millions of years ago, and move to its present location. The original landmass, Mauritia[[8]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lemuria#cite_note-8) and the supercontinent [Gondwana](app://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gondwana "Gondwana") prior to that, broke apart; it predominantly did not sink beneath sea level.

## Theosophy and occultism

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Lemuria&action=edit&section=6 "Edit section: Theosophy and occultism")]

The idea of Lemuria was later incorporated into the philosophy of [Theosophy](app://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theosophy_\(Blavatskian\) "Theosophy (Blavatskian)") and has persisted as a theme in [pseudoarchaeology](app://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudoarchaeology "Pseudoarchaeology") and discussions of [lost lands](app://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_lost_lands "List of lost lands"). There is a vast fringe literature pertaining to Lemuria and to related concepts such as the [Lemurian Fellowship](app://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lemurian_Fellowship "Lemurian Fellowship") and other things "Lemurian". All share a common belief that a [continent](app://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continent "Continent") existed in what is now either the [Pacific Ocean](app://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_Ocean "Pacific Ocean") or the [Indian Ocean](app://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Ocean "Indian Ocean") in ancient times and claim that it became submerged as a result of a geological cataclysm. An important element of the mythology of Lemuria is that it was the location of the emergence of complex knowledge systems that formed the basis for later beliefs.

The concept of Lemuria was developed in detail by [James Churchward](app://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Churchward "James Churchward"), who referred to it as [Mu](app://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mu_\(mythical_lost_continent\) "Mu (mythical lost continent)") and identified it as a lost continent in the Pacific Ocean. Churchward appropriated this name from [Augustus Le Plongeon](app://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Augustus_Le_Plongeon "Augustus Le Plongeon"), who had used the concept of the "Land of Mu" to refer to the legendary lost continent of [Atlantis](app://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlantis "Atlantis"). Churchward's books included _The Lost Continent of Mu, the Motherland of Men_ (1926), _The Children of Mu_ (1931), _The Sacred Symbols of Mu_ (1933), _Cosmic Forces of Mu_ (1934), and _Second Book of Cosmic Forces of Mu_ (1935). The relationships between Lemuria/Mu and Atlantis are discussed in detail in the book _[Lost Continents: The Atlantis Theme in History, Science, and Literature](app://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lost_Continents "Lost Continents")_ (1954) by [L. Sprague de Camp](app://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L._Sprague_de_Camp "L. Sprague de Camp").

Blavatsky claimed that Australia was a remnant inland region of Lemuria and that [Aboriginal Australians](app://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aboriginal_Australians "Aboriginal Australians") and [Aboriginal Tasmanians](app://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aboriginal_Tasmanians "Aboriginal Tasmanians") (which she identified as separate groups) were of Lemurian and Lemuro-Atlantean origin, after cross-breeding with animals. Her idea was subsequently developed in pseudo-histories and fiction of the white Australian popular culture of the 1890s and early 1900s, including the writings of nationalist Australian poet [Bernard O'Dowd](app://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernard_O'Dowd "Bernard O'Dowd"), author [Rosa Campbell Praed](app://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rosa_Campbell_Praed "Rosa Campbell Praed") in _My Australian Girlhood,_ author [John David Hennessey](app://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_David_Hennessey "John David Hennessey") in _An Australian Bush Track_ and [George Firth Scott](app://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Firth_Scott "George Firth Scott")'s novel _The Last Lemurian: A Westralian Romance._[[9]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lemuria#cite_note-:0-9)[[10]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lemuria#cite_note-10)

Robert Dixon suggests that the popularity of the idea of "lost races" like Lemurians and Atlanteans reflected the anxieties of colonial Australians, that "when Englishness is lost there is nothing to replace it".[[9]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lemuria#cite_note-:0-9) [A. L. McCann](app://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A._L._McCann "A. L. McCann") attributes Praed's use of the Lemuria trope to an "attempt to create a lineage for white settlers without having to confront the annihilation of Indigenous people".[[11]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lemuria#cite_note-11)

In 1894, Frederick Spencer Oliver published _[A Dweller on Two Planets](app://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_Dweller_on_Two_Planets "A Dweller on Two Planets")_, an occult book which claimed that survivors from Lemuria were living in a complex of tunnels beneath the mountain of [Mount Shasta](app://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Shasta "Mount Shasta") in northern [California](app://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California "California"). This city, known as _Telos: City of Light_ boasted fur-lined carpeted floors and jeweled walls, all signs of opulence. Spencer also claimed that Lemurians could be seen walking the surface in white robes.[[12]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lemuria#cite_note-12) In 1931, [Harvey Spencer Lewis](app://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvey_Spencer_Lewis "Harvey Spencer Lewis"), who went by the pseudonym [Wishar Spenle Cerve](app://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wishar_Spenle_Cerve "Wishar Spenle Cerve")[[13]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lemuria#cite_note-13)[[14]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lemuria#cite_note-14) wrote _Lemuria: the Lost Continent of the Pacific_, which popularized the idea that Shasta was a repository for Lemurians.[[15]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lemuria#cite_note-15)

In the 1930s, [Guy Warren Ballard](app://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guy_Warren_Ballard "Guy Warren Ballard") claimed to have been approached by [Saint Germain](app://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Count_of_St._Germain "Count of St. Germain") who told him he could endow him with knowledge and wisdom. Ballard wrote and published the book _Unveiled Mysteries_ under the alias Godfré Ray King, where Ballard claimed to be the person that Saint Germain was speaking through to get to the world. The belief in Telos has been proliferated by Ballard and his [followers](app://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Germain_Foundation "Saint Germain Foundation"), as well as other religious groups like the [Ascended Masters](app://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ascended_master "Ascended master"), the [Great White Brotherhood](app://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_White_Brotherhood "Great White Brotherhood"), [The Bridge to Freedom](app://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Bridge_to_Freedom "The Bridge to Freedom"), [The Summit Lighthouse](app://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Summit_Lighthouse "The Summit Lighthouse"), [Church Universal and Triumphant](app://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_Universal_and_Triumphant "Church Universal and Triumphant"), and [Kryon](app://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kryon "Kryon").[[16]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lemuria#cite_note-16)[_[citation needed](app://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")_]

Every year, members of these religious groups make pilgrimage to Mount Shasta, a journey that is marked by various yearly festivals and events. The Saint Germain Foundation hosts the annual "I AM COME!" Pageant, on the Life of Jesus the Christ in Mt. Shasta. The [Rainbow Family](app://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rainbow_Family "Rainbow Family") hosts a _Rainbow Gathering_ every August to commemorate the pilgrimage.[[17]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lemuria#cite_note-17)[[18]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lemuria#cite_note-:1-18) These religions are often a mix of spiritual practices, based largely on native, Christian, Buddhist and Taoist traditions, synthesizing their beliefs, and excluding "negative" aspects of such religions. For example, the [Saint Germain Foundation](app://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Germain_Foundation "Saint Germain Foundation")[[18]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lemuria#cite_note-:1-18) does not include [Jesus' crucifixion](app://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jesus'_crucifixion "Jesus' crucifixion") in their teachings.

[![](app://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/f0/Kumari_Kandam_map.png/250px-Kumari_Kandam_map.png)](app://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Kumari_Kandam_map.png)

"Lemuria" in [Tamil nationalist](app://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tamil_nationalism "Tamil nationalism") mysticist literature as Kumari Kandam, connecting [Madagascar](app://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madagascar "Madagascar"), [South India](app://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_India "South India"), and Australia, covering most of the Indian Ocean

Some Tamil writers such as [Devaneya Pavanar](app://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Devaneya_Pavanar "Devaneya Pavanar") have associated Lemuria with [Kumari Kandam](app://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kumari_Kandam "Kumari Kandam"), a legendary sunken landmass mentioned in the Tamil literature, claiming that it was the [cradle of civilization](app://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cradle_of_civilization "Cradle of civilization"). A Tamil commentator, [Adiyarkunallar](app://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adiyarkunallar?action=edit&redlink=1 "Adiyarkunallar (page does not exist)"), described the dimensions that extended between the [Pahrali River](app://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pahrali_River "Pahrali River") and the [Kumari River](app://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kumari_River?action=edit&redlink=1 "Kumari River (page does not exist)") in the [Pandyan](app://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pandya_dynasty "Pandya dynasty") country that was taken over by the ocean later on.[[19]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lemuria#cite_note-19)
