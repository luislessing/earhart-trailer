In [popular culture](app://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Popular_culture "Popular culture") and [UFO conspiracy theories](app://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UFO_conspiracy_theories "UFO conspiracy theories"), **men in black** (**MIB**) are allegedly [government agents](app://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_agents "Government agents") dressed in dark [suits](app://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suit "Suit"), who question, interrogate, harass, and threaten [unidentified flying object](app://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unidentified_flying_object "Unidentified flying object") (UFO) witnesses to keep them silent about what they have seen. The term is also frequently used to describe mysterious men working for unknown organizations, as well as various branches of government allegedly tasked with protecting government UFO secrets or performing other strange activities. They are typically described as tall men with expressionless faces, slightly [pale skin](app://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pale_skin "Pale skin"), and usually wearing black suits with black [sunglasses](app://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunglasses "Sunglasses"). "Men In Black" encounters are very common tales told in American UFO conspiracy theories.

The term is generic, as it is used for any unusual, threatening or strangely behaved individual whose appearance on the scene can be linked in some fashion with a UFO sighting.[[1]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Men_in_black#cite_note-1)

Stories about men in black inspired the [science fiction comedy](app://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Science_fiction_comedy "Science fiction comedy") franchise [_Men in Black_](app://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Men_in_Black_\(franchise\) "Men in Black (franchise)") and an [album](app://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Gospel_According_to_the_Meninblack "The Gospel According to the Meninblack") by [the Stranglers](app://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Stranglers "The Stranglers").

Folklorist [James R. Lewis](app://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_R._Lewis_\(scholar\) "James R. Lewis (scholar)") compared accounts of men in black with tales of people encountering [Lucifer](app://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lucifer "Lucifer"), and speculated that they can be considered a kind of "psychological trauma".[[2]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Men_in_black#cite_note-Lewis1995-2)

Men in black feature prominently in [ufology](app://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ufology "Ufology"), UFO folklore, and fan fiction. In the 1950s and 1960s, ufologists adopted a conspiratorial mindset and began fearing they would be subject to organized intimidation in retaliation for discovering "the truth of the UFOs".[[3]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Men_in_black#cite_note-Gulyas2016-3)

In 1947, Harold Dahl claimed a man in a dark suit warned him not to discuss his alleged [UFO sighting on Maury Island](app://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maury_Island_incident "Maury Island incident"). In the mid-1950s, ufologist [Albert K. Bender](app://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albert_K._Bender "Albert K. Bender") claimed he was visited by men in dark suits who threatened and warned him not to continue investigating UFOs. He maintained that the men were secret government agents tasked with suppressing evidence of UFOs. Ufologist [John Keel](app://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Keel "John Keel") claimed to have had encounters with MIB and referred to them as "[demonic](app://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demon "Demon") supernaturals" with "dark skin and/or 'exotic' facial features". According to ufologist [Jerome Clark](app://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jerome_Clark "Jerome Clark"), reports of men in black represent "experiences" that "don't seem to have occurred in the world of [consensus reality](app://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consensus_reality "Consensus reality")".[[4]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Men_in_black#cite_note-Slate-4)

Historian Aaron Gulyas wrote: "During the 1970s, 1980s and 1990s, UFO conspiracy theorists would incorporate the Men in Black into their increasingly complex and paranoid visions."[[3]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Men_in_black#cite_note-Gulyas2016-3)

Keel has argued that some MIB encounters could be explained as entirely mundane events perpetuated through folklore. In his book _[The Mothman Prophecies](app://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Mothman_Prophecies "The Mothman Prophecies")_ (1975), he describes a late-night outing in 1967, where he was mistaken for an MIB while searching for a phone to call a tow truck.[[5]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Men_in_black#cite_note-mothman_prophecies-5)

In his article "Gray Barker: My Friend, the Myth-Maker", John C. Sherwood claims that, in the late 1960s, at age 18, he cooperated when [Gray Barker](app://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gray_Barker "Gray Barker") urged him to develop a hoax—which Barker subsequently published—about what Barker called "blackmen", three mysterious UFO inhabitants who silenced Sherwood's pseudonymous identity, "Dr. Richard H. Pratt".[[6]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Men_in_black#cite_note-6)

Songs that reference the urban legend include "E.T.I (Extra Terrestrial Intelligence)" by the [Blue Öyster Cult](app://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_%C3%96yster_Cult "Blue Öyster Cult") (1976),[[7]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Men_in_black#cite_note-7) "Meninblack" by [The Stranglers](app://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Stranglers "The Stranglers") (1979),[[8]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Men_in_black#cite_note-8) "Men in Black" by [Frank Black](app://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Francis "Black Francis"), and "Men in Black" by [Running Wild](app://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Running_Wild_\(band\) "Running Wild (band)") (1995).[[9]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Men_in_black#cite_note-9) In 1981, The Stranglers also released a concept album, _[The Gospel According to the Meninblack](app://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Gospel_According_to_the_Meninblack "The Gospel According to the Meninblack")_.[[10]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Men_in_black#cite_note-twomey-10)

### Film and television

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Men_in_black&action=edit&section=5 "Edit section: Film and television")]

- _[The Alien Encounters](app://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Alien_Encounters "The Alien Encounters")_ (1979) features the Men in Black who harass a UFO investigator.[[11]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Men_in_black#cite_note-11)

- In _[The Brother from Another Planet](app://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Brother_from_Another_Planet "The Brother from Another Planet")_ (1984), two Men in Black try to capture the alien protagonist.[[12]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Men_in_black#cite_note-12)

- The 1997 science-fiction film _[Men in Black](app://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Men_in_Black_\(1997_film\) "Men in Black (1997 film)")_, starring [Will Smith](app://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Will_Smith "Will Smith") and [Tommy Lee Jones](app://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tommy_Lee_Jones "Tommy Lee Jones"), was loosely based on _[The Men in Black](app://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Men_in_Black_\(comics\) "The Men in Black (comics)")_ [comic book](app://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comic_book "Comic book") series created by [Lowell Cunningham](app://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lowell_Cunningham "Lowell Cunningham") and [Sandy Carruthers](app://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sandy_Carruthers "Sandy Carruthers").[[13]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Men_in_black#cite_note-hughes-13) Also that year, the television film _[The Shadow Men](app://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Shadow_Men_\(1997_film\) "The Shadow Men (1997 film)")_ presented a more serious take on the concept.[[14]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Men_in_black#cite_note-14) _Men in Black_ received an [animated spin-off series](app://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Men_in_Black:_The_Series "Men in Black: The Series"), which is set in a separate continuity, and a series of live-action sequel films set in the first film's continuity.

- The Men in Black appear in _[The X-Files](app://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_X-Files_\(franchise\) "The X-Files (franchise)")._[[15]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Men_in_black#cite_note-15)

- In the animated _[Doctor Who](app://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_Who "Doctor Who")_ serial _[Dreamland](app://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dreamland_\(Doctor_Who\) "Dreamland (Doctor Who)")_ (2008) the Men in Black are androids controlled by a shadowy unseen group known as the Alliance of Shades. This story has them hide the truth about [grey aliens](app://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grey_aliens "Grey aliens") at [Area 51](app://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Area_51 "Area 51"). They appear again in _[The Sarah Jane Adventures](app://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Sarah_Jane_Adventures "The Sarah Jane Adventures")_ sequel _[The Vault of Secrets](app://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Vault_of_Secrets "The Vault of Secrets")_ (2010) where they keep a hyperdimensional vault hidden.

- The Men in Black appear in _[The Masked Singer](app://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Masked_Singer_\(American_TV_series\) "The Masked Singer (American TV series)")_. They serve as the security personnel and backup dancers for the contestants and assist in the contestants' unmasking.
    - _[The Masked Dancer](app://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Masked_Dancer_\(American_TV_series\) "The Masked Dancer (American TV series)")_ had a counterpart of the Men in Black called the Women in Black.

- The video game franchise _[Half-Life](app://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Half-Life_\(video_game\) "Half-Life (video game)")_ features a character known as the G-Man, widely regarded as a [pastiche](app://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pastiche "Pastiche") of the concept.[[16]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Men_in_black#cite_note-16)[[17]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Men_in_black#cite_note-17)
- The Men in Black feature in _[Deus Ex](app://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deus_Ex_\(video_game\) "Deus Ex (video game)")_ as agents of [Majestic 12](app://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Majestic_12 "Majestic 12").[[18]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Men_in_black#cite_note-18)
- In _[XCOM: Enemy Unknown](app://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/XCOM:_Enemy_Unknown "XCOM: Enemy Unknown")_, the “Thin Men” are humanoid alien infiltrators posing as human agents wearing suits and sunglasses, behaving in a manner reminiscent of the Men in Black encounters.[[19]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Men_in_black#cite_note-19)
- In [tabletop role-playing game](app://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tabletop_role-playing_game "Tabletop role-playing game") _[Delta Green](app://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delta_Green "Delta Green")_, both [Majestic 12](app://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Majestic_12 "Majestic 12") and Delta Green are inspired by Men in Black conspiracy folklore, with the former representing an [alien-focused covert conspiracy](app://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UFO_conspiracy_theories "UFO conspiracy theories") and the latter originally serving as the government’s Men in Black–style response to supernatural and [Cthulhu Mythos](app://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cthulhu_Mythos "Cthulhu Mythos") threats.[[20]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Men_in_black#cite_note-20)
- In _[Revelations: Persona](app://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revelations:_Persona "Revelations: Persona")_, enemies known as the “Men in Black” (localized as “Blackman” in the original English release) appear as cybernetically enhanced agents serving as hostile security forces.

The [SCP Foundation](app://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SCP_Foundation "SCP Foundation") Wiki is a community world building project that features a group of interest simply called Unknown, which appear as lone men in black suits who's actions occasionally assist or run counter to the SCP Foundations goal of keeping anomalous activity away from society. Despite how extensive the lore of the SCP universe has become, Unknown's allegiance and motives remain undefined, similar to the mythical forces of nature that some MIB encounters are described as.
